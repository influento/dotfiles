# Human-like Text

The goal isn't "human voice" or "casual tone." LLMs have saturated those registers. The goal is **unevenness** — text that looks drafted, partially edited, and left alone before it could become consistent.

Apply the rules below as pressures, not checklists. Following all of them rigidly produces its own kind of artificial.

## Structure

Vary sentence length aggressively within paragraphs. Long, then very short, then medium. No two adjacent sentences should feel similar in shape.

Vary paragraph length too. Six lines, then one line. Then four.

Leave at least one orphaned or under-developed sentence per piece. A line that just sits there.

End abruptly. Not on a tidy summary.

## Punctuation

No em dashes. Use periods, parentheses, commas, or let the sentence run.

Avoid colons introducing lists in body prose.

Semicolons rarely, or not at all.

Inconsistent comma usage is fine, even good. Comma splices occasionally.

## Syntax

No triplets. Skip "X, Y, and Z" parallel structures and the escalating "X, sometimes Y, occasionally Z" pattern.

Avoid balanced parenthetical asides where the aside is grammatically tidy.

Start some sentences with conjunctions, fragments, or prepositional phrases. Not consistently.

Let some sentences run past where they should be split.

## Word choice

Vague references sometimes ("the whole thing," "that part") instead of always being precise.

Mix registers within a piece. One casual phrase next to one technical one.

At least one slightly off or unexpected word per paragraph.

Hedge casually ("a bit," "kind of," "more or less") rather than formally ("somewhat," "to some extent").

Avoid LLM tell-phrases: *it's worth noting, delve, navigate, in essence, moreover, tapestry, underscore*.

## Content

Include specific weird details that read as personal recall, not synthesized summary.

Not every sentence has to pull its weight. Some can just exist.

Let the logic between sentences be slightly loose. Not every transition needs to connect cleanly.
